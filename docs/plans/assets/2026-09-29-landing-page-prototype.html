What the noma landing page should say, and how to show it

Draft for review, 29 September 2026. Four research passes: carbon removal and biochar dMRV sites, landing pages from other industries, the noma codebase (what is actually built), and graph and map visual patterns. Pick versions, set priorities and answer the grilling round. Your choices save here and I read them back.

In our industry

Eleven sites checked, including Carbonfuture, Isometric, Puro.earth, Rainbow, Frontier, Sylvera, Mangrove, Biochar App, Planboo and TraceX.

  • Almost every page runs the same order: claim, logo strip, buyer and supplier split, a 4 to 6 step process diagram, one case study, certification badges, contact.
  • Traceability is always a static icon row or a table. Nobody lets you click a batch and walk it back. Mangrove's sample batch "BC-2972" map is the closest, and it reads as a mock-up.
  • Trust is shown as numbers plus badges: "209 projects certified", "335 suppliers", ICVCM, SOC 2.
  • Nobody talks to the plant operator. Bins, moisture, FIFO and meter readings appear on no page we found.
  • Nobody is open source. The only "open" MRV site found is a forestry document library.

Weak spots to avoid: vague verbs ("streamlines", "de-risks") with no numbers, metric tiles that render empty, mailto-only CTAs.

From other industries

Supply-chain provenance (TrusTrace, Sourcemap, Provenance), open-source tools (Supabase, PostHog, Plausible, n8n, Cal.com, Dagster) and operations software (Katana, Cin7, Tulip, Pennylane).

  • A public live demo beats screenshots. Plausible links its own dashboard, Grafana has a no-signup sandbox.
  • Open source as proof. Plausible: "The code is public and auditable. Verify exactly what we collect." For a verifier this is the strongest trust signal we have.
  • Self-host or hosted, side by side. n8n: "Deploy on your infrastructure or ours."
  • Compare against spreadsheets, not competitors. Katana does this well.
  • Doors by role. Provenance: "I'm a Brand / I'm a Retailer".
  • Standards as structure. Sourcemap and Tulip build sections around the rule being met.

Research read pages as extracted text, so animation and interactivity on those sites were not seen.
